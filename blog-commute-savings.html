Petrol vs. Electric: How One Bangalore Commuter Saved ₹58,000

An audited 12-month financial comparison between a conventional 125cc petrol scooter and the EcoRide Pulse across 11,200 km of city commuting.

A
Ananya Sharma Urban Mobility Analyst
📅 Oct 08, 2026 • ⏱ 5 min read • ✓ Engineering Verified
Urban commuter scooters staged along an arterial transit link; real riders logging 35-50 km daily across metropolitan traffic zones.

⚡ Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • Fuel savings alone delivered ₹46,200 in net cash retained over 11,200 km.
  • Elimination of engine oil, oil filters, spark plugs, and carburettor/injector servicing saved an extra ₹8,400.
  • Home overnight charging cost just ₹0.14 per km compared to ₹2.65 per km on standard petrol.
  • Full initial vehicle capital purchase price difference was fully amortized in under 16 months.

With metropolitan traffic congestion worsening and petrol prices fluctuating between ₹102 and ₹108 per liter, daily commuting on internal combustion engine (ICE) two-wheelers has become an increasingly painful monthly drain on household budgets.

To quantify the exact financial advantage of going electric, we tracked Vikram R., an IT consultant commuting 38 kilometers daily round-trip between Whitefield and Koramangala in Bangalore, over a strict 12-month evaluation period.

1. The 12-Month Audited Expense Ledger

Over the course of 312 working days plus weekend errands, Vikram clocked exactly 11,480 kilometers. Below is the audited breakdown comparing his prior 125cc Japanese ICE scooter with his current EcoRide Pulse commuter scooter.

"In year one alone, the savings paid for my annual family vacation, two new premium helmets, and my home solar inverter upgrade."

2. The Reality of Domestic Electricity Tariffs

Riders often ask: Will charging an EV blow up my monthly residential electric bill? The answer is straightforward math.

The EcoRide Pulse features a 3.4 kWh lithium-ion battery pack delivering an honest real-world range of 115 km on a single charge. Accounting for AC-to-DC onboard charging conversion losses (approx. 10%), a full 0% to 100% recharge consumes approximately 3.74 kWh (units) of electricity.

  • At standard metropolitan tier-2 residential tariffs of ₹7.50 per unit, a full 115 km charge costs exactly ₹28.05.
  • That equals an astonishing ₹0.24 per kilometer at peak rates, or ₹0.14 per km if charged under discounted EV off-peak nocturnal tariffs available across major state distribution companies.
  • Compare this with a petrol scooter consuming ₹2.60 to ₹2.90 per kilometer in stop-and-go gridlock!

3. Elimination of Moving Mechanical Parts

A typical internal combustion 4-stroke engine contains over 200 moving parts—pistons, crankshafts, valves, spark plugs, timing chains, variator rollers, and transmission clutches. Every one of these parts generates frictional heat and degrades engine oil, requiring quarterly workshop visits.

By contrast, our direct-drive permanent magnet motor has exactly one moving assembly: the rotor spinning smoothly on sealed Japanese ceramic bearings. There is no engine oil to drain, no radiator coolant to flush, no transmission belt to snap, and zero exhaust catalytic converter to foul up with soot.
